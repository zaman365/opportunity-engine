# Design mandate · an evidence desk, not another AI SaaS dashboard

**New product-design work.** The designer is responsible for the interface's authorship, not for decorating a preselected component library. Build software that an experienced product team would be proud to use daily: clear, distinctive, rigorous and unusually well finished.

## 1. The experience we are commissioning

A working environment for deciding what is true, what matters, and what to do next. The product should feel like a carefully designed professional instrument—part research desk, part review room—not a lead-generation dashboard with an “AI” badge.

**Convention governs mechanics. Distinctiveness governs expression.** Keep navigation, forms, keyboard behavior and feedback learnable. Find originality in information hierarchy, evidence presentation, composition, typography, pacing and the relationship between observation and action.

“Silicon Valley quality” is a craft benchmark, not a visual style or instruction to copy Linear, Notion, Stripe, Vercel or any other company. A clone with our logo is not the requested outcome.

## 2. Product truth that must survive every concept

An operator selects an approved page. The system performs a bounded inspection. A reviewer sees real evidence and decides whether a finding is supported. A customer receives a clear report and a deliverable service scope. Permission, uncertainty, source context and cost remain visible.

The product's signature object is **the evidence-backed decision**, not the chat message, KPI tile or generic project card. A screenshot is primary working material, not a decorative image behind a translucent panel.

## 3. Fixed versus open

| Fixed responsibilities | Designer must define |
|---|---|
| Evidence, finding and commercial hypothesis are distinct | The best composition to make that distinction effortless |
| Truthful empty/loading/partial/blocked/stale states | Their visual hierarchy, microcopy, transitions and recovery behavior |
| Task journey, authority boundaries and accessibility | Navigation expression, density modes, panel proportions and responsive adaptation |
| Existing venture identity on customer surfaces | A coherent shared operator identity and how brand context appears without restyling the entire workspace |
| No false metrics, generated proof or fabricated product photography | How authentic artifacts, annotations and data carry visual character |
| Production state is not prototype fiction | How the design lab and live product remain unmistakably separate |

The proposed routes and HTML reference communicate jobs and state—not a compulsory pixel layout. A better composition is welcome when it demonstrably improves review and stays within the contracts.

## 4. The actual problems to solve

**Fragmentation:** a list, screenshot, interpretation, quote and delivery task can become unrelated screens. Keep the selected account and question continuous across the journey.

**False certainty:** most automated audits visually overstate confidence. Make scope and evidence quality legible without burying the task under warning banners.

**Homogeneous dashboards:** four KPI cards, a chart, colored badges and a table say nothing about this product. Every primary element must earn its place through a real operator decision.

**Over-boxing:** nested cards and redundant toolbars flatten importance. Prefer meaningful sections, alignment and rules; use a container only when it expresses a real boundary or interaction.

**Hidden context:** opening a finding should not require repeated navigation to recover the screenshot, variant, observed date or reviewer history. Preserve working context and scroll position.

## 5. Creative territories to explore

Explore at least two, preferably all three, on **the same populated opportunity and review action**. Changing palettes or border radii is not a different concept.

### A · Evidence Desk — recommended starting territory

An editorial research surface: a compact case index, generous artifact stage, numbered evidence references, a precise observation column and a quiet review seam. Typography and alignment create authority. Tables are compact where comparison matters; the selected artifact gets space. A short, distinctive docket/case identifier links feed, detail, report and activity.

Why it fits: the central task is reading proof and making a judgment. Risk: editorial character can become an attractive static document; prove efficient keyboard review, long data and dynamic states.

### B · Inspection Instrument

A more technical environment organized around test conditions, reproducibility, coverage and a synchronized evidence timeline. Emphasize diagnostic state without imitating a code editor. A narrow parameter strip and a contextual inspection canvas replace the conventional sidebar/card grid.

Why it fits: strong for MarktFix and later authorized diagnostics. Risk: too intimidating for reviewers or customers. Keep labels understandable and explanations progressive.

### C · Review Studio

A visually led side-by-side review space for real product-page evidence, alternate product states and an evolving deliverable brief. Use carefully controlled composition and image prominence, not a Pinterest grid or generic creative whiteboard.

Why it fits: strong for PDP Studio and creative diagnosis. Risk: overemphasizing visual criticism while hiding reproducibility, costs or permissions. Prove those controls remain findable.

### Choosing

Compare task clarity, evidence/context continuity, uncertainty comprehension, keyboard/mobile behavior and original character. Record tradeoffs. Provisional local default is A; do not stall implementation awaiting routine aesthetic confirmation. Obtain owner/design review before a broad production visual freeze.

## 6. What originality should look like here

Develop two or three useful, ownable ideas rather than twenty decorative effects. Candidates to investigate—not all mandatory:

**Evidence-to-claim linking:** selecting a numbered reference highlights its actual artifact area and corresponding assertion. Hover may preview, but click and keyboard must fully work.

**Review seam:** a persistent yet quiet strip joins source state, unsaved decision, reason and next action. It belongs to the selected finding rather than an unrelated global toolbar.

**Case continuity:** one discreet identifier and observation title carry from queue to evidence to protected report. The user knows where they are without a giant breadcrumb stack.

**Honest coverage:** express “2 of 5 pages captured; size-guide check complete” in context. Do not replace nuanced coverage with a universal health ring.

**Changed-since-review:** visibly compare the prior approved evidence to the new observation. Re-review is a meaningful action, not a flashing badge.

Judge every idea by whether it helps real work. It is acceptable to reject all candidate signatures and propose stronger ones with clear rationale.

## 7. Visual principles

Use neutral white and cool near-white surfaces, near-black text, and restrained status/focus color. Do not fall back to fashionable beige/stone minimalism. Color is allowed where it earns semantic value; black-only is not a requirement for interactive focus/error legibility. No accent-color wallpaper, neon dark canvas, glowing gradients, glassmorphism or “agent brain” decoration.

Design typography as a system: distinguish navigation, section titles, case titles, findings, evidence captions, metadata, numerical values and customer narratives. A premium typeface alone is not authorship. Never use microscopic pale labels to simulate sophistication. Explore an expressive display voice only where it improves hierarchy; dense work remains readable.

Create a deliberate density rhythm. The review queue should support scanning; the evidence stage needs room; the report should read like a considered professional assessment. Do not use the same card skeleton everywhere. Whitespace must separate decisions, not inflate the number of scrolls.

Corners, rules, shadows and icon weights need a rationale. Use shadows for actual layering; do not add one to every region. At most two meaningful surface levels per screen unless a dialog requires a third. Roundedness is not the design concept.

## 8. Components and libraries

Accessible headless primitives and proven interaction foundations are encouraged. **Unmodified UI-kit composition is not.** Replace defaults that determine the visual identity, not accessibility behavior that already works. Do not rebuild dialogs, focus trapping or screen-reader semantics for originality's sake.

Use a consistent icon family sparingly and only when it clarifies action. Custom evidence/status markers may become a distinctive vocabulary, but icon-only controls need accessible names and visible context. Do not decorate every heading with an unrelated icon.

Create components from the selected real screen, not by filling a UI-kit checklist. See COMPONENTS.md for required behavior; visual form belongs to the designer.

## 9. Motion and interaction craft

Motion should explain selection, expansion, preservation of context and completion. Use short transitions, provisionally 140–220 ms, with reduced-motion support. Never animate the content people are trying to inspect. No artificial typing, looping scan activity, fake progress bars or celebratory confetti for a paid/billing action.

Optimistic updates are allowed only for safely reversible preferences. Human review, cost authorization and report publication show pending/confirmed/error accurately. Preserve typed notes across version conflicts. Loading skeletons should match real layout and carry a status label, not impersonate evidence.

## 10. Responsive and accessible by design

Desktop is the primary production workspace; mobile must still support review, inspect, reject/approve and follow-up, not merely show “use desktop.” Recompose panels into a readable sequence or tabs at narrow widths; do not shrink the desktop layout. Preserve current case/decision when moving between evidence and action.

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Text contrast, focus visibility, focus not obscured, non-color status cues, zoom/reflow, keyboard navigation and meaningful labels are acceptance criteria. W3C's 24 CSS-pixel minimum target criterion has defined spacing/exceptions; our design target is normally 40 px desktop actions and 44 px touch actions where practical. Do not call those product targets universal WCAG requirements. See SOURCES.md [S3,S4,S11].

Disable single-letter shortcuts by default or make them configurable and inactive inside text inputs. Provide an ordinary accessible route for every keyboard shortcut and drag gesture.

## 11. Required design deliverables

1. A one-page concept rationale and two/three genuine composition studies using identical content.
2. A chosen interaction prototype for intake → scan → evidence → review → report, including at least empty, partial, blocked, stale and conflict states.
3. Desktop and narrow-screen layouts with long German copy, realistic URLs and constrained screenshots.
4. Tokens, component/state inventory, interaction notes and actual microcopy—not only pristine happy-path frames.
5. A documented three-pass critique with revisions and actual browser screenshots.
6. A handoff decision log explaining how the result is specific to this product and what remains provisional.

When using Figma or another design tool, use editable layers/components, descriptive names, auto-layout/constraints, styles and documented states. Never claim a Figma node/file was created without an actual tool result. When using code, provide real runnable interactions and CSS tokens. No fake external integrations to make a prototype feel complete.

## 12. Critique gates

**Pass 1 — composition:** hide the logo. Can the core evidence/review job still be recognized? Is hierarchy authored or just a kit? Does the artifact have the right prominence?

**Pass 2 — task usability:** have someone find the strongest evidence, distinguish a hypothesis, handle a partial scan and make a reversible review decision. Note hesitation and fix it. In the absence of a participant, record this as a heuristic walkthrough, not a user test.

**Pass 3 — differentiation and craft:** remove decoration that does not support the product. Inspect alignment, truncation, spacing, contrast, keyboard behavior, mobile continuity and error recovery. Compare to generic defaults and state the substantive differences.

Reject a design that becomes generic when its logo is removed, obscures evidence behind decorative containers, omits hard states, or trades accessibility for novelty. Conversely, do not reject good familiar mechanics merely because they are familiar.

## 13. What this kit's preview is—and is not

`preview.html` is one authored Evidence Desk exploration with self-contained styling and local interactive behavior. All cases are synthetic. The evidence images are browser-rendered captures of the original local fixture responses; HTTP status was checked separately. Direct browser navigation to the local fixture host was blocked in the authoring environment. They are not scans of real merchants. It proves a direction and supplies useful content pressure; it is not a completed multi-screen platform or a substitute for the required design exploration.

No font binaries are included. The reference uses system fonts. Any chosen production font must be acquired and licensed through the project's own approved channels, tested for language coverage and performance, and served according to that license.
